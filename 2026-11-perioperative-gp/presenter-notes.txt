PERIOPERATIVE MEDICATION OPTIMISATION: FIVE GP DECISIONS
Audience: Australian general practitioners. Duration: 15 minutes.
Prepared and sources checked: 17 November 2026.

DELIVERY
Intro: 45 seconds.
Each of five case/question pairs: 2 minutes 30 seconds (12 minutes 30 total).
  Read the case with its animated clues: 20 seconds.
  Advance to the question, read the options and vote: 40 seconds.
  Reveal the explanation and discuss the practical decision: 90 seconds.
Closing checklist: 1 minute 45 seconds.
References: supporting material; do not read aloud.

There are exactly five MCQs and 13 steps. Four options per question; correct
positions are B, D, A, C, B. Cases are fictional educational examples.

Use each case-reading slide before its voting question. The underlines identify
evidence, without presenting the correct option. Explanations are hidden until
the teacher reveals results. Reveal and discuss before leaving a question:
Fimbria closes voting permanently when the teacher advances away from it.

Keep the discussion to one decision per case:
1. GLP-1: translate the anaesthetic diet/fasting plan into clear instructions.
2. Apixaban: turn relative days into actual last-dose and restart instructions.
3. Biologics: implement an agreed specialist plan, balancing infection and flare.
4. SGLT2: identify a fasting-related risk that a normal glucose does not exclude.
5. ACE inhibitor/ARB: check the indication and avoid a blanket rule.

SCOPE AND CASE BOUNDARIES
This is an elective adult noncardiac surgery lesson, not an emergency surgery
algorithm. Reconcile the examples with the hospital's instructions. The GP's
role is to identify gaps, coordinate the plan and make it understandable.

The anticoagulation case specifies therapeutic-dose apixaban, preserved renal
function, high bleeding risk and no neuraxial technique. Do not reuse its
calendar for warfarin, another DOAC or a spinal/epidural. Recent thrombosis,
mechanical valves or complex antithrombotic combinations merit specialist input.

The rheumatoid arthritis case explicitly states that the treating team has
already chosen an interruption. That avoids implying that every Australian
patient receiving a biologic needs it withheld. The methotrexate example assumes
normal renal function and blood counts. Other agents, especially rituximab and
JAK inhibitors, require their own schedule; do not extrapolate adalimumab timing.

The SGLT2 calendar is intentionally tied to morning empagliflozin and a Friday
inpatient operation. If the patient uses a combination tablet, identify both
ingredients so any alternative glycaemic plan covers the other ingredient too.

For ACE inhibitor/ARB discussion, emphasise that preventing hypotension and
improving major clinical outcomes are different claims. The answer offers a
reasonable option for agreement with anaesthesia, not a mandatory stop order.
