# Urinary excretion comparison: sources and definitions

The chart uses **percentage of administered dose recovered as unchanged parent drug in urine**, not percentage of total systemic clearance. Those metrics are not interchangeable when oral bioavailability is incomplete. It does not graph total urinary radioactivity, a dose reduction percentage, dialysis removal or kidney injury risk. This scope covers the named psychiatric agents in the lesson plus common psychiatric/neurological comparators. Transplant and cardiovascular case medicines are outside this first comparison.

Sources are primary prescribing documents and human PK papers. Values come from different studies, collection intervals, formulations and populations and should be treated as approximate teaching comparisons, not a head-to-head experiment. An inequality is shown as an inequality, not an exact value.

## Interpretation

Venlafaxine, risperidone and bupropion have relevant active metabolites. Mirtazapine clearance is reduced in renal impairment despite low unchanged urinary recovery. Duloxetine also has restrictions in severe renal impairment despite <1% unchanged urinary recovery. These examples prevent a low parent percentage from becoming a misleading renal safety ranking.

Baclofen’s plotted value is explicitly calculated from its SmPC: 75% urinary recovery × 95% unchanged = 71.25%, displayed as about 71%. Quetiapine’s <1% is an upper bound because its label does not allocate total unchanged excretion between urine and feces. Bupropion’s unchanged excretion is likewise not resolved by route in the cited label and is shown as text only. Diazepam is described qualitatively in its source.

Gabapentin, metformin and digoxin have different denominators and are described separately on the overview rather than merged into its bars. Their values and denominator notes remain in the JSON/CSV.
